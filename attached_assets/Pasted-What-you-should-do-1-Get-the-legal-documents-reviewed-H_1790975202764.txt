What you should do
1. Get the legal documents reviewed
Have a solicitor familiar with UK SaaS, GDPR, and health data review:
Privacy Notice
Terms of Use
Subscription and cancellation terms
Refund policy
Client-data responsibilities
PARQ/health-data handling
Data deletion and export
Liability and service availability
Governing law and company details
The current pages explicitly say they should be reviewed by a solicitor before public launch, so that wording should not remain on a polished public release.
2. Resolve the health-data and GDPR position
Because the product can store PARQ and health-form responses, clarify:
Who is the data controller and who is the processor
What lawful basis applies to each type of data
How special-category health data is handled
How long data is retained
What happens when an account is deleted
Where data is hosted
Which subprocessors are used
Whether data leaves the UK/EEA and what safeguards apply
Whether you need a Data Processing Agreement for coach customers
How data breaches are reported and handled
You should also ask the solicitor whether a DPIA or other formal assessment is appropriate.
3. Confirm your vendors and subprocessors
Your current Privacy page names services such as:
Replit Auth
PostgreSQL
Brevo
Stripe
Confirm the actual provider, location, role, and current DPA/processing terms for each. Do not state that regions or agreements are “not yet confirmed” once you launch publicly.
4. Make the product copy match reality
Review statements about:
GoCardless
Direct debit
Stripe billing
Client payments
Invoices
PARQ forms
Email notifications
Data export and deletion
If a feature is not enabled, say “planned,” “available when configured,” or remove the claim. The marketing page, FAQ, Terms, Privacy Notice, and dashboard should all tell the same story.
5. Decide whether you are still in beta
If you are genuinely running a private beta, you can keep the modal, but change it to clearly say something like:
“Practably is currently in private beta. Please review the current beta Terms and Privacy Notice before using the service.”
If you are launching publicly:
Replace the beta Terms and Privacy Notice with final versions.
Remove “owner review before public launch.”
Remove or rewrite the beta warning.
Update the acceptance modal with the final effective date.
Keep records of the version each user accepted.
Provide a way to notify users when the Terms materially change.
6. Test the acceptance flow
After the legal copy is finalized, verify that:
Users cannot access protected features without accepting the current Terms.
The acceptance is timestamped and associated with the correct document version.
Users can read both Terms and Privacy information before accepting.
Existing users are prompted again when the legal version changes.
Declining or closing the modal behaves clearly.
Acceptance does not accidentally imply consent to marketing or health-data processing beyond what the Privacy Notice explains.
Practical launch decision
You have two sensible options:
Private beta now
Keep the product private, retain the warning, use test or invited users only, and clearly label the service as beta.
Public launch later
Do not launch until the legal documents, health-data handling, vendor agreements, and product claims are reviewed and finalized.
The immediate next step is to give your solicitor the current Terms, Privacy Notice, product feature list, data-flow/vendor list, and screenshots of the acceptance modal.
